Scanning finds your files. **Enrichment** is the separate background pass that gives them posters,
descriptions, ratings, genres and release dates. A freshly scanned library is playable
immediately and looks bare for a while.

## The providers

| Provider | Needs a key? | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| AniList | No | Anime |
| TMDB | Yes — `BEAM_TMDB_API_TOKEN` | Film and TV generally |

AniList is enabled by default and needs no credentials, so a stock install already enriches anime.
TMDB requires a token; **without one, titles that would have matched TMDB are simply left
un-enriched.** Scans do not fail over it, and nothing in the UI breaks — you just get no artwork
for those titles.

<Aside type="caution" title="A default install talks to AniList">
Because `BEAM_ANILIST_ENABLED` defaults to true, a Beam server contacts `graphql.anilist.co`
during enrichment even if you never configure TMDB. Set it to false if you want a server that
contacts nothing but your identity provider.
</Aside>

## Why a title has no artwork

In rough order of likelihood:

1. **Enrichment has not reached it yet.** It works through titles in batches.
2. **No TMDB token**, and the title is not anime.
3. **The title Beam parsed does not match anything.** Beam searches with the name it derived from
   your filename, so scene noise or an unusual layout produces a query that finds nothing. Check
   how the title appears in the UI — if it reads oddly, the filename is why. See
   [Managing libraries](/operate/libraries/#how-beam-decides-what-a-file-is).
4. **The match scored below the confidence threshold**, `BEAM_ENRICH_MIN_CONFIDENCE`. Beam prefers
   no metadata to wrong metadata.

A match replaces the title's displayed name, year and artwork with the provider's. It does not
change how Beam groups files: later files of the title still join it by the name their filename
parses to, however the provider spells it. See
[Managing libraries](/operate/libraries/#multiple-versions).

The admin **Status** tab shows each title's enrichment state, so you can tell "not tried yet"
apart from "tried and found nothing", and whether each provider is configured: TMDB shows as
`not_configured` until `BEAM_TMDB_API_TOKEN` is set, and a provider that is set but whose client
could not start shows as `unavailable` (the server log says why).

`GET /v1/admin/enrichment?status=unmatched` (or `failed`) lists the titles to fix, newest first,
each with the reason it was left that way.

## Pinned titles

A provider id in an `.nfo` beside your media **pins** the title to it (see
[NFO files](/operate/libraries/#nfo-files-container-tags-and-subtitles)). Enrichment then works
in this order:

1. A match an administrator chose for the title comes first (see [Fixing a wrong match](#fixing-a-wrong-match)).
   It is itself a pin, the administrator's, fetched by its id.
2. A **TMDB or AniList** pin, for a provider you have configured, is fetched directly by its id —
   no search, no confidence threshold. This is the fix for a title search keeps getting wrong:
   write its id into an NFO.
3. Anything else is searched for as usual. An **IMDb or TheTVDB** pin — or a pin for a provider
   you have not configured — is checked against the match: a match that carries a different id of
   the pinned provider is rejected and the title is left unmatched, with the reason in its
   enrichment state. A match carrying no id of that provider cannot be checked, and is kept.

Pinning a title, or editing the NFO to pin it to another id, queues it for enrichment again with
its old match discarded. Re-running enrichment for a pinned title (a rematch) discards its match
and keeps its pin. A pin an administrator sets by matching a title by hand takes precedence over
any NFO: no NFO — edited, or beside a new file — replaces it, so correcting a title never means
editing a file in your library.

## Fixing a wrong match

When search keeps picking the wrong title, fix the match rather than the filename:

1. `GET /v1/admin/media/{id}/match-candidates` lists what the configured providers offer, best
   first, scored as enrichment scores them. Pass `query` (and `year`) to search for something
   other than the title's own name.
2. `POST /v1/admin/media/{id}/match` with `{"external_ref": "tmdb:603"}` fixes it. The title is
   pinned to that id as **your** pin — it outranks any NFO's, and no NFO replaces it — and queued;
   the next pass fetches it by that id, with no search. An id the provider says it has no title
   for is retried with a growing delay, naming the id, and the title is left unmatched once the
   retries run out.
3. `DELETE /v1/admin/media/{id}/match` undoes it: your pin is cleared, the title goes back to
   what an NFO beside its files says or, with none, to being searched for by its name, and it is matched again.

The id must be from a provider you have configured, and one id pins one title: if another title
already holds it, the two are probably the same title — fix the other one first.

## Locking fields

`PUT /v1/admin/media/{id}/enrichment/locks` with `{"locked_fields": ["title", "poster"]}` keeps
those fields exactly as they are through every later enrichment; everything not listed is
unlocked. Lockable: `title`, `original_title`, `description`, `year`, `release_date`, `runtime`,
`poster`, `backdrop`, `rating`, `genres` — a show has no `release_date` or `runtime`. The provider
ids are never locked: they are the match, which you change by fixing it.

## Re-running enrichment

Failures retry automatically with a growing delay. So does a pinned title whose id the provider
answers "not found" for; a title no pin holds whose match the provider no longer has is searched
for again by its name instead. You can also trigger a re-enrich for a title
from the admin area — the right move after fixing a filename, or after adding a TMDB token to a
library that was indexed without one. `POST /v1/admin/libraries/{id}/refresh` re-enriches one
library, and `POST /v1/admin/media/refresh` everything; each answers with how many titles it
queued. The outcome of every title arrives on the admin event stream as an `enrichment` event.

A show's rating is stored when the show is enriched. A show an older Beam enriched — before
shows kept a rating — has none until it is enriched again, so it is left out of a minimum-rating filter and sorts with the unrated titles
until then. Re-enriching everything from the admin area fills them in at once; otherwise they fill
in as titles are refreshed.

## Tuning

`BEAM_ENRICH_BATCH_SIZE` and `BEAM_ENRICH_INTERVAL_SECS` control throughput.
`BEAM_METADATA_LANGUAGE` selects the language of returned metadata **from TMDB only** — AniList has
no equivalent setting. Raising throughput too far risks provider rate limits, which cost you more
time than the smaller batch would have.

## Artwork and viewer privacy

**Beam serves artwork itself.** Your server fetches each poster, backdrop and episode still from
the provider once, stores it, and serves it to your viewers from its own cache. Nobody's browser,
phone or TV ever connects to TMDB or AniList, so neither provider can see who is browsing what —
or that your server exists at all beyond the enrichment lookups it makes on its own behalf.

This replaces the earlier behaviour, where Beam handed out provider CDN links and every viewer's
device connected to them directly. If you run Beam precisely so that nothing leaves your network,
that gap is closed
([ADR-0015](https://github.com/justin13888/beam/blob/master/docs/architecture/decisions/ADR-0015-artwork-served-by-beam.md)).
No Beam cookie or credential is ever sent to a provider, in this or any other request.

The cache lives in `BEAM_DATA_DIR/artwork` and is capped by `BEAM_ARTWORK_CACHE_MAX_BYTES`
(1 GiB by default); once it is full the least recently shown images are dropped and re-fetched if
they are needed again. Setting it smaller than your library's artwork costs a little bandwidth,
never a broken poster. It needs no pruning or invalidation of its own: when enrichment finds
different artwork for a title, that is a different image, and the old one ages out on its own.

Two things follow that are worth knowing:

- **Artwork now uses your server's bandwidth.** If viewers watch from outside your network, the
  posters they see travel from your uplink rather than from a CDN. Posters are small and clients
  cache them hard, so this is usually unnoticeable — but it is not zero on a metered connection.
- **Downloaded titles do not yet show artwork offline.** The Android app caches the images it has
  shown, but does not deliberately fetch a title's poster when you download it, so a title
  downloaded and then opened with no connection may show a placeholder. Tracked in
  [#152](https://github.com/justin13888/beam/issues/152).
