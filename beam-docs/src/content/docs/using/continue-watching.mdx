---
title: Resume and history
description: Beam remembers where you stopped and what you have watched, without you saving anything.
---

Beam records your position as you watch. There is nothing to press — it reports periodically
during playback, so closing the tab, losing power or having the browser crash still leaves you
close to where you were.

## Continue watching

Titles you are partway through appear in a continue-watching row on the home page, so the thing
you are most likely to want next is the first thing you see. The row holds one entry per movie and
one per show, most recently played first:

- **A movie** you stopped partway through, to resume.
- **A show** at the episode to watch next: the one you stopped partway through, or — once you
  finish an episode — the next one, even across the end of a season. Episodes you have already
  watched, and episodes Beam has no file for, are stepped over; one you had already started
  resumes where you left it. When you are caught up, the show leaves the row.

Finish a movie and it drops off the row. To take something off the row yourself, remove it: it
stays away until you next play it, and keeps your place meanwhile.

Beam builds the row from the 1,000 titles you played most recently, counting shows you have
finished. Something you are partway through but have not touched since playing a thousand other
titles falls outside that and leaves the row; it keeps your place, and playing it again brings it
back.

## Resuming

Reopening a partly-watched title picks up where you stopped rather than starting from the
beginning. If you would rather start over, seek back — the resume point is a starting position,
not a restriction. You can also clear it, which forgets where you were without forgetting that
you have watched the title before.

## Watched and unwatched

Watching to within the last 5% of a title marks it **watched** and puts its resume point back at
the start. Rewinding afterwards does not unmark it — that is a rewatch, and it resumes like any
other title. Beam counts how many times you have watched each one.

You can also mark a movie, an episode, a whole season or a whole show watched without playing it,
or unwatched to forget it entirely. Marking a show watched marks the episodes it has now; an
episode added later is unwatched.

Title pages show what you have watched: each movie and episode says whether it is watched and where
you stopped, and each season and show how many of its episodes you have seen.

## Watch history

The history page lists every movie and episode you have watched or started, newest first.

## Three things that surprise people

- **Your position is yours alone.** Every user's progress is tracked separately, so watching
  something on a shared server does not affect anyone else's resume point.
- **Your place follows the title, not the file.** Switching to a different quality version of the
  same title keeps your place, and Beam remembers which version you last chose and offers it
  again while it is still there.
- **A file that goes offline leaves the row — for a while.** If the disk holding the only file of a
  title is disconnected, the title leaves continue-watching and history. Your position is kept, and
  comes back with the file when the disk does. If another version of the title is still there,
  the row offers that one instead.
