## Outcome

Android drains the core's durable progress queue at app start and whenever connectivity returns, so a resume point recorded offline reaches the server. The core's contract says the queue is "Called on reconnect and at app start". A test pins both triggers.

## Findings

- **android-IMPL-003 / android-TEST-003** `beam-android/core/ffi/src/main/kotlin/dev/beam/android/core/ffi/repository/Repositories.kt:202`, `BeamRepositories.kt:194`. `PlaybackRepository.flushProgress` has no production caller. `grep -rn flushProgress beam-android`, excluding tests and `core/testing`, finds only the interface and the adapter. The core documents the call as made "on reconnect and at app start" (`beam-client-core/src/ffi.rs:1444`). Apple calls it.
- `pendingProgressCount` and `ServerRepository.sessionState` also have no production caller (same grep). `AdminRepository.health/logs/events/refreshMetadata` are unused too, but #322 is adopting them, so they stay.

## Decisions taken

These are the run's decisions under the autonomy contract. Nobody has reviewed them.

1. Wire `flushProgress`, or delete it.
   Taken: wire it, at process start after `restore` and on every network-available transition (`ConnectivityManager` callback), through a single owner that also runs after `select_server`.
   Rejected: deleting it. Positions recorded offline would then never be sent, which breaks resume across devices (FR-5xx) for anyone who watches offline.
   Reverses: delete the trigger and the test, and file the loss as a known gap.
2. Remove `pendingProgressCount` and `ServerRepository.sessionState`, which have no caller, unless the wiring uses them. A UI badge is not added; that would be a feature.

## Surface

Observable behaviour: offline progress now reaches the server. Nothing else changes.

## Constraints

- No gate is weakened, and no feature is added beyond the documented drain.
